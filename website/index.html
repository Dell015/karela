The Problem

The Philippines has a health and civic challenge no existing app was built to solve.

Not because the problem is hidden — because the apps solving it were designed for someone else, somewhere else.

On the health side

Movement without meaning

Lifestyle-related disease — diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular illness — is rising sharply, driven by sedentary behaviour. Yet people already walk significant distances daily: to tricycle stops, across campuses, between markets. No system acknowledges any of it, so none of it feels like progress.

On the civic side

Bayanihan with no digital form

The Philippines is among the most disaster-affected countries on Earth. The same communities that walk past flooded drainage and damaged roads daily are the ones most harmed when those issues go unreported. Volunteer effort is uncoordinated, unverified, and invisible to the local governments that need data to respond.

On the motivational side

Built for the wrong user

Strava is built for competitive athletes. Nike Run Club is a product of a shoe brand. Pacer is passive. None understand the commuter, the student org volunteer, or the disaster preparation worker. None are designed for low-end Android, prepaid data, and inconsistent 4G. None generate verifiable civic data.

The primary barrier to physical activity for most Filipinos is not laziness. It is meaninglessness.

How It Works

Two engines, fused by one physical state.

Karela runs two parallel tracks that are deeply interconnected. The layer joining them is what makes it more than the sum of its parts.

Track 01

Fitness Intelligence

An adaptive ghost pacing system that models and predicts individual fatigue in order to improve exercise adherence — not to celebrate peak performance.

  • Per-user effort decay curve learned from run history
  • Rolling 4-week composite baseline, anomaly-resistant
  • GPS Kalman filtering and spatial interpolation
  • Sensor fusion anti-cheat: GPS plus accelerometer

Why this matters

The system never asks a runner at their physical limit to stop and photograph a drain. It reserves that request for the moment the user is already slowing down and has the bandwidth to spare. Civic contribution becomes adaptive rather than forced.

The Ghost System

You race the version of yourself you can actually beat.

Every other app builds its ghost from your personal best. But a peak run represents exceptional conditions, not sustainable effort — so you end up racing a version of yourself you can rarely match. That is a discouragement engine.

Static PB ghost (what other apps use) Karela adaptive ghost Your actual pace
P(t) = P_baseline × e^(−λ × max(0, t − t_fatigue)) The per-user effort decay function. λ is your individual decay rate and t_fatigue your fatigue onset — both learned from your own run history, not assumed from a population average.
1

Spatial interpolation

Urban GPS is unreliable — signal bounces off buildings, drops in tunnels, jumps indoors. Karela smooths positional data across waypoints and fills continuity gaps so the ghost never teleports and per-segment distance stays accurate.

2

Effort decay modelling

Rather than treating fatigue as a universal constant, Karela learns where your pace drops, how fast it deteriorates, and whether the trigger is terrain, time, or effort. Slow down 15 minutes into flat road runs? That is read as a pacing inefficiency, not a fitness ceiling — and the ghost paces you slower early to extend your strong window.

3

Reinforcement-style calibration

After every session the ghost re-evaluates: did you beat your checkpoints, did decay start earlier or later than predicted, are you accumulating fatigue or gaining fitness? It nudges forward when you consistently beat it, and pulls back when you consistently miss.

The ghost's goal is to stay slightly ahead of your historical failure point — not your peak performance.

Meet Ani

A coach who knows your body, your city, your week.

Every fitness app has coaching. What they share is that it is generic — the same 5 km suggestion for a 55 kg student and a 90 kg office worker, the same rest-day advice whether you slept four hours or eight, the same notification whether it is typhoon season in Cagayan Valley or not.

Ani

● Reading your context

Good morning, Rina! It's 7:30 AM in Tuguegarao — light winds, 26°C. Perfect for your morning route. Your 11-day streak is one of your best. Let's make it 12.

Feeling a bit sore today

Noted. Your resting step rate suggests you might be carrying fatigue, so I've swapped today's quest for a light 800 m — consistency matters more than distance right now. Your streak still counts.

There's also a drainage node two streets from your usual route that needs reconfirming. One tap as you pass, no stopping. Only if you're up for it.

Illustrative conversation, written from the §16 specification

1

Quests calibrated to you

A sedentary user is never handed a 5 km run on day one — Ani starts at 500 m and scales from completion history. Targets sit at roughly 110% of your comfortable current output, never a generic fixed standard.

2

Body-aware coaching

Ani reads height, weight, age, fitness baseline, rolling 7-day step average, streak status, completion rate, local weather, and your city. Every message is built from that context.

3

A plan every Sunday

Seven days of distance targets, one rest day, one challenge day, one civic quest — adjusted mid-week if your completion rate drops or a Bayanihan event activates.

Ani is honest about her limits

Ani is a language model with general wellness knowledge — not a licensed nutritionist, physiotherapist, or doctor. She does not prescribe caloric deficits, diagnose injury, or override a stated medical condition. Mention an injury during setup and she defaults to low-impact quests while recommending you consult a health professional.

The Civic Engine

You are a sensor node, not a janitor.

Early designs asked runners to stop and clean or report during runs. That was identified as a fatal UX flaw — nobody wants to break flow state for maintenance work. So the model inverted: the collective movement of runners across a city becomes a human sensor array, and the heavy verification happens server-side.

Three independent reports within the radius and time window promote a pending node to a verified civic quest.

Pending → Verified → Aging → Expired ↺ Reconfirmed resets decay
1

Reporting stays frictionless

A quick photo and a single tap to categorise — trash, flooding, drain blockage, damaged infrastructure, unsafe area. GPS, timestamp, and device orientation attach automatically. Nothing else is asked of you.

2

Consensus kills fake reports

If civic reports earn XP, people will game them. So verification is a DBSCAN-inspired clustering pass run server-side through PostGIS: three independent reports of the same category, inside a 15–30 m radius, within a time window. One user cannot self-verify, duplicates count once, and isolated false positives never reach threshold.

3

Confidence decays over time

A flooded drain gets fixed. Trash gets cleared. So every verified node carries a confidence score that decays, tuned per category — structural damage decays slower than litter. A runner passing by taps "Still there" and the score resets. Stale data cannot pollute the map, and LGUs can trust what they receive is current.

C(t) = C₀ × e^(−μ × (t − t_verified)) Temporal decay of node confidence. μ is tuned per issue category. Reconfirmation resets C(t) to C₀ = 1.0.
The Bayanihan Protocol

When the storm comes, the app stops asking you to run.

This is the feature that turns Karela from a personal health tool into civic infrastructure — a verified volunteer coordination system producing exportable data for local governments during and after disaster events. And its most important rule is a hard limit on itself.

The Safety Hard-Lock — Ani as ethical gatekeeper

Tier 0 · Normal No advisory, no admin flag All features active.
Tier 1 · Watch Wind > 40 km/h or rain > 10 mm/hr Outdoor missions flagged with caution. Ani suggests indoor alternatives.
Tier 2 · Warning PAGASA Signal 2–3 or admin pre-disaster Outdoor missions suspended. Only indoor Preparation Quests active. Ghost paused. Emergency banner shown.
Tier 3 · Hard Lock PAGASA Signal 4–5 or admin emergency lock All movement rewards disabled. GPS tracking suspended. Only Safety Check-in and Request Assistance remain visible. Gems are awarded for staying safe.
Tier 4 · Recovery Admin marks zone yellow or green Recovery Quests unlock in cleared zones only. Proof of Impact required for every civic mission.

Karela must never be the reason someone gets hurt

The Safety Tier system is non-negotiable and overrides every other function in the app. At Tier 3 there is no way to earn movement rewards at all — the incentive to go outside is removed entirely, by design.

Proof of Impact — multi-factor verification

Civic XP is only worth something if it cannot be faked. Five independent checks stand between a submission and a reward.

01

In-app camera only

Gallery uploads are blocked, so old photos cannot be submitted.

02

Before & after capture

Dual-capture split-screen is enforced for cleanup and fortification quests.

03

Metadata fingerprint

GPS, altitude, device ID, and a server-generated timestamp — never the device clock.

04

Vanguard blind audit

Three independent Vanguards from outside your Guild must all approve.

05

Rotating QR at major missions

A Node Master QR rotating every 15 minutes, scanned on arrival and departure.

Note on PAGASA: no public REST API currently exists. Tier 0–1 escalates automatically from weather data; Tier 2–4 requires a Karela admin or verified LGU coordinator to escalate manually using official PAGASA bulletins. A webhook partnership is a later roadmap milestone.

Progression

Consistency pays more than intensity.

Every level costs a flat 1,000 XP, so progression never suffers power creep. What changes is your multiplier — and it rewards showing up, not going hard.

Breaking a streak resets the multiplier to 1.0× immediately. The 3.0× cap is deliberate — no one can exceed triple base XP, which keeps the game about moving rather than about maximising multipliers.

A dual currency, split by purpose

XP measures progress and can never be spent. Gems are earned in flat amounts and are the only spendable currency — which keeps progression honest and prevents a pay-to-win dynamic.

Why Not Just Use Strava?

Because it was built for a different person.

Strava, Nike Run Club, and Pacer are excellent apps for the users they were designed for. That is the honest answer — and it is also the point.

Feature comparison between mainstream fitness apps and Karela
Question Strava · Nike Run Club · Pacer Karela
Who is the target user? Competitive runners, cyclists, athletes Everyday commuters, students, civic volunteers

Karela is not for elite athletes. Most fitness apps celebrate the person who ran a sub-four-minute kilometre. They ignore the student who walked forty-five minutes across campus every day for a year. Karela is built for that student.

Built for the Philippines

Designed for prepaid data, mid-range Android, and typhoon season.

These are not afterthoughts bolted onto a Western app. They are the constraints the architecture started from.

Local-first, cloud-sync

All sensor data and run tracking happens offline in SQLite, then syncs to Supabase when connectivity returns. Every event carries a UUID so sync is idempotent.

Runs on the phone you own

Low RAM footprint and batch syncing, tested against mid-range Android rather than flagship hardware. Data cost is treated as a real constraint, not an edge case.

RA 10173 by design

Raw GPS is processed on-device and never uploaded as a full trail. Accelerometer data is discarded after use. Up to five 100 m Privacy Zones are never stored, not even locally. Account deletion completes within 72 hours.

Piloting in Tuguegarao

The beachhead is university students and young professionals in Tuguegarao City, Cagayan Valley — dense, reachable through campus channels, and socially motivated by community recognition.

Civic XP counts equally

A Debris Clearer after a typhoon earns recognition on the same footing as a runner. Civic contribution is tracked separately and valued equally — not treated as a side quest.

Bayanihan, made legible

The cultural value of communal mutual aid finally gets a digital expression — verified, coordinated, and visible to the local governments that need the data.
