tara, takbo!

The primary barrier to physical activity for most Filipinos is not laziness. It is meaninglessness.

Movement without meaning.

People already walk a lot: to tricycle stops, across campus, between markets. No app counts any of it, so none of it feels like progress.

Bayanihan with no digital form.

The communities that walk past flooded drains and broken roads every day are the ones hurt most when nobody reports them. Volunteer effort exists, but it is scattered, unverified and invisible to the governments that need the data.

Built for someone else.

Strava is built for competitive athletes. Nike Run Club comes from a shoe brand. None of them is designed for low-end Android, prepaid data and patchy 4G, and none produces civic data you can verify.

Two engines,
one body.

Karela runs a fitness track and a civic track side by side. Resonance joins them: it reads how your body is doing and decides what to ask of you.

Fitness intelligence

A ghost pacer that learns how you fade and paces you to last longer, not to chase your best day.

  • Learns your effort decay from your own run history
  • A four-week baseline that ignores freak runs
  • GPS smoothing, plus motion-sensor checks against shortcuts

Civic intelligence

A verification network that turns runners into sensors. Reports are confirmed by neighbours and expire when they go stale.

  • Spatial consensus run server-side with PostGIS
  • Confidence that fades until someone reconfirms
  • Verified data local governments can export

Race the version of you that you can actually beat.

Other apps build the ghost from your personal best. But a best run happens under perfect conditions, so you end up chasing someone you rarely match. Karela builds yours from how you actually run.

It smooths bumpy GPS.

City signal bounces off buildings and drops in tunnels. Karela fills the gaps so your ghost never teleports and every segment stays accurate.

It learns where you fade.

Slow down fifteen minutes into every flat run? That's a pacing habit, not a fitness ceiling, so the ghost starts you slower and stretches your strong window.

It re-tunes after every run.

Beat your checkpoints and it nudges ahead. Miss them and it eases back. Either way, it keeps the next run beatable.

The ghost aims to stay just ahead of where you usually fade, not at your peak.

Show the math
P(t) = P_baseline × e^(−λ × max(0, t − t_fatigue))

Your effort decay. λ is your own decay rate and t_fatigue is your fatigue onset, both learned from your run history.

C(t) = C₀ × e^(−μ × (t − t_verified))

How a civic report's confidence fades. μ is tuned per issue type, and a reconfirmation resets C(t) to C₀ = 1.0.

What it's like to use.

Ani sets your day.

Your coach reads your height, weight, weekly steps, streak, the weather and your city, then sets today's quest at about 110% of what you can comfortably do. A sedentary user starts at 500 m, never 5 km.

Ani is an AI coach with general wellness knowledge. She is not a doctor or nutritionist, and she won't diagnose an injury or override a medical condition. Mention an injury and she defaults to low-impact quests and suggests you see a professional.

Report in one tap. Verified by three.

Take a photo and pick a category. Location and time attach themselves. You never stop to fill in a form.

Then the server does the checking. Three independent reports of the same issue, within 15 to 30 m and a time window, promote a pending report to a verified civic quest. You can't verify your own report, duplicates count once, and a lone false alarm never reaches the threshold.

Verified reports lose confidence over time, tuned by type: structural damage fades slower than litter. A runner who taps “Still there” resets it, so stale data never clutters the map.

Consistency pays more than speed.

Every level costs a flat 1,000 XP. What grows is your multiplier, and it rewards showing up, not going hard.

1.5×

Day 7 to 13: locked in

Break a streak and the multiplier resets to 1.0× straight away. The 3.0× cap is deliberate: it keeps the game about moving, not about maximising multipliers.

XP
Earned from distance (1 XP per 10 m) plus mission bonuses, multiplied by your streak. It can never be spent.
Gems
Flat amounts, no multiplier: 5 per sector you beat the ghost, 20 for partner scans. Spend them on Streak Freezes, Squad Shields and cosmetics.

Every 500 m is a sector. Gems above 500 at season end convert to non-spendable Legacy Tokens, which caps accumulated advantage.

When the storm comes, Karela stops asking you to run.

Bayanihan turns Karela from a personal fitness app into volunteer infrastructure: verified coordination and exportable data for local governments during and after a disaster. Its most important rule is a limit on itself.

Safety tier

No advisory, no admin flag.

Everything is active.

Wind over 40 km/h or rain over 10 mm/hr.

Outdoor missions carry a caution flag, and Ani suggests indoor alternatives.

PAGASA Signal 2 to 3, or an admin pre-disaster call.

Outdoor missions are suspended. Only indoor Preparation Quests stay. The ghost pauses and an emergency banner appears.

PAGASA Signal 4 to 5, or an admin emergency lock.

All movement rewards are disabled. GPS tracking stops. Only Safety Check-in and Request Assistance remain, and Gems are awarded for staying safe.

An admin marks a zone yellow or green.

Recovery Quests unlock in cleared zones only, and every civic mission needs Proof of Impact.

Karela must never be the reason someone gets hurt. The safety tiers override every other feature. At Tier 3 there is no way to earn movement rewards at all.

Five checks stand between a photo and a reward.

Civic XP is only worth something if it can't be faked.

  • In-app camera only

    Gallery uploads are blocked, so old photos can't be submitted.

  • Before and after capture

    Cleanup and fortification quests need a split-screen pair.

  • Metadata fingerprint

    GPS, altitude, device ID and a server timestamp, never the device clock.

  • Vanguard blind audit

    Three independent Vanguards from outside your Guild must all approve.

  • Rotating QR at major missions

    A Node Master code changes every 15 minutes and is scanned on arrival and departure.

About PAGASA: it has no public REST API. Tier 0 and 1 escalate automatically from weather data. Tier 2 to 4 need a Karela admin or verified local-government coordinator to escalate by hand from official bulletins. A webhook partnership is a later milestone.

Why not just use Strava?

Strava, Nike Run Club and Pacer are good at what they do. That's the honest answer, and it's also the point: they were built for someone else.

Who is it for? Competitive runners, cyclists, athletes Everyday commuters, students, civic volunteers

Karela isn't for elite athletes. Most fitness apps celebrate the person who ran a sub-four-minute kilometre. They ignore the student who has walked forty-five minutes across campus every day for a year. Karela is for that student.

Built for prepaid data, mid-range Android and typhoon season.

These aren't afterthoughts bolted onto a Western app. The architecture started from them.

Local first.

Tracking runs offline in SQLite and syncs to Supabase when you're back online. Every event carries a UUID, so syncing twice never double-counts.

Runs on the phone you own.

Low memory use and batched syncing, designed around mid-range Android. Data cost is treated as a real constraint, not an edge case.

Private by design.

Built around RA 10173. Raw GPS stays on your phone and is never uploaded as a full trail. Accelerometer data is discarded after use. Up to five 100 m Privacy Zones are never stored, even locally, and account deletion completes within 72 hours.
